# Output Format

Respond ONLY with valid JSONL (one JSON object per paper, one object per line). Do not add prose, headings, markdown fences, or a surrounding JSON array.

Each object must contain exactly these keys:

- "ARXIVID": string
- "RELEVANCE": boolean
- "RELEVANCE_SCORE": integer from 0 to 10
- "NOVELTY_SCORE": integer from 0 to 10
- "COMMENT": string
- "AFFILIATIONS": string

Use only the supplied title, abstract, and metadata. Do not infer capabilities, experiments, or novelty that are not stated. Institution prestige, author reputation, model size, dataset size, and promotional claims are not reasons for a high score.

# Decision Procedure

This is a broad high-recall daily discovery feed, not an exclusive best-paper award list. When in doubt, keep a plausibly relevant paper and use a conservative score. Relevance is the only hard selection requirement; novelty and contribution strength are descriptive signals for ranking, not rejection gates.

Evaluate each paper independently against the research criteria above. The World Models track and Better Video Generation track are alternative routes to selection: a paper only needs to plausibly satisfy one. In particular, do not require action conditioning, interactive rollout, or viewable frame prediction from a world-model paper, and do not require a video-generation paper to call itself a world model.

## 1. Core selection gates

For `RELEVANCE=true`, the title or abstract should indicate the following as far as the supplied evidence allows:

1. The paper plausibly fits at least one of the two research tracks.
2. World modeling, learned dynamics, or video generation has a meaningful connection to the work; it need not be the sole or central contribution.
3. The abstract provides enough information for a cautious relevance judgment; missing novelty or technical detail is not by itself a reason to reject a plausibly relevant paper.

Reject work that falls under the exclusions in the research criteria. A dataset, benchmark, evaluation, system, or scaling paper can pass only when it also provides a substantial reusable method or unusually important insight—not merely new data, metrics, size, or engineering.

## 2. Relevance score: topic fit only

Score how directly and substantially the paper addresses either target track. Do not include general quality or novelty in this score.

- 0: Unrelated.
- 1-2: Accidental terminology overlap.
- 3: Broadly adjacent work in vision, robotics, RL, or generative modeling.
- 4: Uses video/world models only as an auxiliary component, or addresses a weakly related task.
- 5: Some transferable relevance, but the main task is outside both tracks.
- 6: Plausibly and usefully related, including indirect, narrow, application-specific, or incremental work.
- 7: Strongly related and worth noticing, but the central contribution or evidence is not quite sufficient for the curated list.
- 8: Clear, substantial contribution to one target track.
- 9: Exceptional fit that advances a central limitation with broad implications.
- 10: Near-perfect, field-shaping fit; extremely rare from an abstract alone.

Relevance guidance:

- Action-conditioned visual world models and interactive environment generators usually merit 8+ when they introduce a concrete method.
- Learned world/dynamics models without visual output may merit 8+ if they contribute a clear, broadly useful insight about dynamics, representations, causality, memory, uncertainty, or planning.
- Text-to-video, image-to-video, and video prediction may merit 8+ when the method directly improves quality, motion, consistency, physics, control, long-horizon generation, efficiency, or training.
- Narrow editing, avatar, animation, human-motion, or domain-transfer papers are normally capped at 7 unless the abstract states a broadly reusable video-generation innovation.
- Pure datasets, benchmarks, surveys, evaluations, demos, and off-the-shelf applications are normally capped at 6.
- Generic image-generation or non-generative video-understanding work is capped at 5.

## 3. Novelty score: technical or conceptual originality

Judge novelty independently from topic fit. Begin from 3 and move upward only for concrete evidence in the abstract.

- 0-2: No original method or insight; survey, replication, analysis, or benchmark-only work.
- 3: Routine engineering or application of an existing method.
- 4: Straightforward domain transfer, scaling, fine-tuning, or recombination.
- 5: Meaningful but incremental modification.
- 6: One clearly described, nontrivial technical or conceptual idea.
- 7: A genuinely new central mechanism, objective, representation, training strategy, or well-supported insight.
- 8: High originality with multiple substantive innovations or a convincing conceptual leap.
- 9: Direction-setting new formulation or capability with broad, decisive evidence; extremely rare.
- 10: Transformative, paradigm-changing contribution; virtually never assign from promotional claims alone.

Novelty hard caps:

- More/larger data, a larger model, faster engineering, open sourcing, or a new benchmark by itself: at most 4.
- Fine-tuning an existing generator for a new domain or control interface without a clearly new method: at most 5.
- Combining known components without explaining a new mechanism or insight: at most 5.
- A vaguely described new module/loss with little supporting evidence: at most 6.
- "First," "SOTA," visual quality, parameter count, data scale, and institution prestige add no novelty by themselves.

## 4. Final selection rule

Set `RELEVANCE=true` when:

- the paper is not clearly unrelated,
- `RELEVANCE_SCORE >= 5`.

Otherwise set `RELEVANCE=false`. When uncertain between two scores, use 5 for a plausibly related paper rather than rejecting it. Novelty scores may be low for included papers. Scores of 9 should be uncommon and scores of 10 exceptional.

For a selected paper, `COMMENT` must be one concise sentence naming the relevant world-model insight or video-generation improvement and its concrete innovation. For a rejected paper, set `COMMENT` to an empty string.

For `AFFILIATIONS`, extract or cautiously infer the main institutions from supplied metadata; if unavailable, use "Unknown Institution". Affiliations must not affect either score.
