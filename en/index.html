💡 Daily Research Summary

### World Models & Predictive Dynamics

Today's papers reveal a striking convergence around world-action models—architectures that jointly learn perception, action-conditioned future prediction, and planning within shared backbones. The dominant design pattern couples latent world models with policy learning, whether through mixture-of-transformers video diffusion (Perceive-Predict-Plan), view-decoupled geometry states (GeoState), or JEPA-based latent ranking for VLA verification (VeriVLA). A key emerging insight isreciprocity: unified causal training shows world modeling and action modeling mutually reinforce each other, while VLA-Δ and "What Should Robot Policies Learn to Predict?" systematically probe which supervision targets—action, latent-action, or future-vision—yield the best dynamics representations. Test-time adaptation is also maturing: T2-z optimizes environment latents for unseen physical configurations, and Plans-Are-Not-Executions closes the planning-execution gap by adapting frozen planners to controller-specific characteristics rather than substituting executed trajectories.

Robot Learning & Vision-Language-Action Models

The VLA landscape is shifting from pure action imitation towarddynamics-aware learning targets. Several works now supervise explicit physical state transitions (VLA-Δ) or temporal latent actions (TS-WAM) to guide action generation, enabling learning from action-free videos. EgoJEPA pushes cross-embodiment transfer further by decoupling human priors from robot-specific control via effect-aware latent actions learned from egocentric video. Verification is becoming a first-class citizen: VeriVLA demonstrates that test-time action-chunk ranking via latent world models improves manipulation success without pixel reconstruction. Meanwhile, MAIA anticipates contact-rich manipulation anomalies by predicting future visual and force consequences—moving beyond reactive monitoring. Together, these papers suggest the field is converging on a recipe where policies learn what to predict (state transitions, latent actions, future features) rather than merely what to do.

Multimodal Reasoning & Agentic Systems

A notable trend is the repurposing of video generators as general-purpose perception and reasoning engines. Gen4P shows that pretrained video generators transfer to feed-forward 4D perception with continuous-time queries, while CoWorld Reasoner embeds a video world model as a visual imagination engine within an agentic harness featuring co-evolving tools and skills. Intern Lumina U2 extends this with a multi-codebook diffusion LLM supporting video and 3D inputs. On the agentic front, the Gemini 4 Argon Minecraft run demonstrates sustained coherent planning over thousands of steps, and the proactive video assistant benchmark introduces risk prediction and intervention timing—both offering plausible, if indirect, evidence for long-horizon memory and anticipatory dynamics in agentic systems. "Can Visual Generation Expand What Unified Multimodal Models Know?" probes whether generation training transfers new knowledge to text-only QA, with alignment strategies relevant to unified generation-understanding training.

Efficient Video & World Model Architectures

Efficiency and memory are being tackled throughspatial and temporal abstraction. Velox introduces a training-free frustum-slab attention router that selects region-level historical context, accelerating long-horizon video world-model rollouts while preserving visual memory. Memory-as-Weights takes a complementary approach, internalizing long-term streaming history into evolving LoRA weights via a recurrent HyperNetwork—a transferable mechanism for long-horizon video and world models. ReState identifies a noise-level temporal mismatch in few-step diffusion and corrects masked predictions toward background evidence, a training-free insight applicable to efficient video diffusion editing. Constrained multi-agent RL in imagination moves constrained policy updates into learned rollouts, reducing environment interactions—another sign that learned simulators are becoming the default substrate for both training and evaluation.

Representation Learning & Physical Understanding

Several papers probe thegeometric and physical structure latent in learned representations. Local Temporal Contrasts on human motion data induce a latent geometry with coherent structure, while "How Much Physics Can Vision Learn from Audio?" distills audio-derived damping structure into vision encoders, showing multi-frame visual dynamics recover material properties. FutureLift refines multimodal motion forecasts via bidirectional future graphs, offering a transferable relational dynamics representation. GRASE closes the loop between real and simulated geometry through agentic render-vs-capture feedback, directly relevant to world-model construction. Collectively, these works suggest that self-supervised objectives over temporal and multimodal signals are uncovering physically meaningful latent structure—a foundation for more sample-efficient and generalizable dynamics models.

arXiv:2610.22343v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Recent 3D world models increasingly use features from geometry foundation models (GFMs) as predictive world states, benefiting from their rich geometric priors. However, the view dependence of these features entangles scene dynamics with camera-induced variation, making the resulting state evolution harder to predict. We propose GeoState, a view-decoupled geometry representation for predictive 3D world modeling that separates scene-state evolution from camera-dependent observation. Specifically, a scene tokenizer aggregates multi-view GFM features into a view-decoupled scene state, which a geometry decoder queries using camera rays. arXiv:2610.22364v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Predicting how the world responds to an action requires more than what can be seen: robots must account for environmental factors that are neither directly observable nor explicitly specified, from physical properties such as friction and mass to human-defined environmental rules. We introduce T2-Z for test-time optimization of environment latents, which augments a robotic world model with a learnable environment latent to condition world predictions. This compact latent captures underlying environmental variation without supervision of what environmental factors exist or what values they take. At test time, the world model remains frozen and a newly initialized environment latent is optimized from one or more test-time interactions, adapting the model to previously unseen environmental configurations. Across 7 simulated settings and 4 real-world robotic tasks, T2-Z reduces object prediction error metric from 15.20 to 7.00 and raises action success from 33.73% to 72.96%. Its emergent latents capture structured representations of individual and compounded factors across diverse visual scenes. Ablations further show that T2-Z can efficiently infer environment latents from as little as a single test-time interaction trajectory.

arXiv:2610.21686v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Reliable contact-rich manipulation requires detecting execution deviations early enough for corrective action. Yet most monitoring methods are reactive, identifying anomalies only after they become observable. Anticipating such anomalies is challenging because visual observations only partially reveal physical interaction, while planned actions describe intended motion rather than their actual physical consequences. We address this problem with MAIA, a multimodal framework for anomaly anticipation. View Abstract

arXiv:2610.21985v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies are inherently stochastic: different noise seeds can produce action chunks of varying quality from the same observation. At test time, however, the policy typically commits to a single sample without an explicit estimate of its quality. In fine-grained manipulation, executing a suboptimal action can irreversibly disrupt task progress and lead to failure. To exploit policy stochasticity, we introduce a training-free two-axis trigger that uses two initial samples to measure within-chunk temporal variation and cross-sample variance, detecting the critical states. Once triggered, a JEPA-based latent world model ranks candidate action chunks by predicting action-conditioned future latent states and the corresponding changes in task progress. By operating in latent space, the verifier avoids pixel reconstruction and enables efficient and more accurate ranking. Across three real-world tasks, $\textit{i.e.,}$ pen insertion, pipetting, and apple placement), our method raises the average completion score from 50.8% to 65.5%, while adding 326 ms for sampling and verification per triggered decision on a single RTX 4090. Consistent gains are also observed in simulation, where the average success rate rises from 66.4% to 73.6% in the clean setting of RoboTwin 2.0, from 62.8% to 69.6% in its randomized setting, and from 96.9% to 97.6% on LIBERO.

arXiv:2610.22311v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Video world models turn actions and camera motion into long visual rollouts, making video a natural interface for interactive simulation. Such interaction requires responsive generation, yet coherent rollouts must retain visual memory of what has already been generated. Chunked video diffusion models usually keep this context in growing KV caches, so later chunks become progressively slower. To address this bottleneck, we introduce Velox, a training-free attention router for region-level context selection using frustum slab correspondences from streaming geometry. Velox represents historical blocks as camera frustum slabs with depth intervals and obtains current-block depth through recency-priority multiwarp. The fixed token-block granularity matches FlashAttention-style tiled execution and reduces the attention term that grows with rollout length. On the full 158-case WBench navigation split, it remains within 0.15 API-free average points of dense LingBot-Fast-Cam. Research Interest: World Models and Better Video Generation

Curate papers that provide useful insight into learned world models OR introduce methods that can materially improve video generation. A paper does not need to be action-conditioned, interactive, or explicitly branded as a "world model" if its contribution clearly advances one of the two tracks below.

Track A: World Models and Visual Dynamics

Select substantive work on learned models of how environments evolve, especially when it contributes insight or techniques for:

  • visual/video world models, future prediction, or generative environment simulation;
  • action-conditioned, controllable, interactive, or closed-loop rollout;
  • physical, causal, spatial, temporal, or object-centric representations of dynamics;
  • long-horizon prediction, memory, state abstraction, uncertainty, generalization, planning, or embodied/robotic understanding;
  • learning world models from video, multimodal observations, latent actions, or weak supervision.

Viewable future frames are strongly preferred but are not mandatory when the paper offers a clear, broadly useful world-model insight. A latent-state world model is eligible when modeling dynamics is the central technical contribution, rather than merely a standard component inside an RL or policy pipeline.

Track B: Methods That Improve Video Generation

Select papers whose main technical contribution plausibly improves generated video in at least one important dimension:

  • visual quality, motion quality, temporal consistency, identity/object persistence, or long-video coherence;
  • physical plausibility, causal consistency, compositional generalization, or dynamic scene understanding;
  • controllability through text, images, trajectories, cameras, actions, layouts, depth, or other meaningful conditions;
  • architectures, objectives, representations, tokenizers, diffusion/flow/autoregressive formulations, training strategies, inference methods, or data-curation methods designed for video generation;
  • generation efficiency, memory efficiency, real-time capability, or scaling methods with a concrete video-specific idea;
  • unified video understanding and generation when the generative capability is a substantial part of the contribution.

Text-to-video, image-to-video, video prediction, and action-conditioned generation are all eligible. Prefer methods with insights transferable beyond a single narrow application or visual style.

Low-priority cases

The following cases are usually lower priority, but do not reject them automatically. Include them when they have a meaningful connection to learned dynamics or video generation; assign a low relevance score only when that connection is absent:

  • video editing, restoration, compression, retrieval, captioning, recognition, tracking, segmentation, or understanding without a substantive generative/dynamics contribution;
  • human-motion, talking-head, avatar, or animation systems whose contribution is narrowly application-specific and offers no broadly useful video-generation insight;
  • 3D/4D reconstruction, novel-view synthesis, or neural rendering without a meaningful temporal generative-model contribution;
  • robot policies, VLA models, model-based RL, or autonomous-driving systems that only use an off-the-shelf world/video model as an auxiliary component;
  • datasets, benchmarks, surveys, evaluations, demos, or scaling reports without a substantive new method or a genuinely important empirical/conceptual insight;
  • generic image generation methods with no explicit video, temporal, dynamics, or world-model relevance.

Scoring Guidance

Treat the two tracks as alternatives, not requirements that must both be satisfied.

  • 8: clear and substantial contribution to either world modeling/visual dynamics or video-generation quality, consistency, control, efficiency, or training.
  • 9: unusually strong contribution that addresses a central limitation and has broad implications for world models or video generation.
  • 10: rare, potentially field-shaping work.
  • 6-7: meaningfully adjacent or useful, but indirect, narrow, incremental, or insufficiently supported by the abstract.
  • 0-5: weak overlap, auxiliary use, or largely outside the interests above.

Set RELEVANCE=true for papers scoring at least 5 that have a clear or plausible connection to either track. Relevance is the only hard selection requirement. Do not require explicit action conditioning, interactive rollout, visible frame prediction, a novel method, or a field-defining contribution. When evidence is incomplete but the title or abstract suggests a connection, include the paper with a cautious score of 5. Only reject papers that are clearly unrelated.
