Research Interest: World Models and Better Video Generation

Curate papers that provide useful insight into learned world models OR introduce methods that can materially improve video generation. A paper does not need to be action-conditioned, interactive, or explicitly branded as a "world model" if its contribution clearly advances one of the two tracks below.

Track A: World Models and Visual Dynamics

Select substantive work on learned models of how environments evolve, especially when it contributes insight or techniques for:

  • visual/video world models, future prediction, or generative environment simulation;
  • action-conditioned, controllable, interactive, or closed-loop rollout;
  • physical, causal, spatial, temporal, or object-centric representations of dynamics;
  • long-horizon prediction, memory, state abstraction, uncertainty, generalization, planning, or embodied/robotic understanding;
  • learning world models from video, multimodal observations, latent actions, or weak supervision.

Viewable future frames are strongly preferred but are not mandatory when the paper offers a clear, broadly useful world-model insight. A latent-state world model is eligible when modeling dynamics is the central technical contribution, rather than merely a standard component inside an RL or policy pipeline.

Track B: Methods That Improve Video Generation

Select papers whose main technical contribution plausibly improves generated video in at least one important dimension:

  • visual quality, motion quality, temporal consistency, identity/object persistence, or long-video coherence;
  • physical plausibility, causal consistency, compositional generalization, or dynamic scene understanding;
  • controllability through text, images, trajectories, cameras, actions, layouts, depth, or other meaningful conditions;
  • architectures, objectives, representations, tokenizers, diffusion/flow/autoregressive formulations, training strategies, inference methods, or data-curation methods designed for video generation;
  • generation efficiency, memory efficiency, real-time capability, or scaling methods with a concrete video-specific idea;
  • unified video understanding and generation when the generative capability is a substantial part of the contribution.

Text-to-video, image-to-video, video prediction, and action-conditioned generation are all eligible. Prefer methods with insights transferable beyond a single narrow application or visual style.

Exclusions

Do not select papers that are primarily:

  • video editing, restoration, compression, retrieval, captioning, recognition, tracking, segmentation, or understanding without a substantive generative/dynamics contribution;
  • human-motion, talking-head, avatar, or animation systems whose contribution is narrowly application-specific and offers no broadly useful video-generation insight;
  • 3D/4D reconstruction, novel-view synthesis, or neural rendering without a meaningful temporal generative-model contribution;
  • robot policies, VLA models, model-based RL, or autonomous-driving systems that only use an off-the-shelf world/video model as an auxiliary component;
  • datasets, benchmarks, surveys, evaluations, demos, or scaling reports without a substantive new method or a genuinely important empirical/conceptual insight;
  • generic image generation methods with no explicit video, temporal, dynamics, or world-model relevance.

Scoring Guidance

Treat the two tracks as alternatives, not requirements that must both be satisfied.

  • 8: clear and substantial contribution to either world modeling/visual dynamics or video-generation quality, consistency, control, efficiency, or training.
  • 9: unusually strong contribution that addresses a central limitation and has broad implications for world models or video generation.
  • 10: rare, potentially field-shaping work.
  • 6-7: meaningfully adjacent or useful, but indirect, narrow, incremental, or insufficiently supported by the abstract.
  • 0-5: weak overlap, auxiliary use, or largely outside the interests above.

Set RELEVANCE=true for papers scoring at least 8 that contain a concrete technical contribution or a genuinely important world-model insight. Do not require explicit action conditioning, interactive rollout, or visible frame prediction. When evidence is ambiguous, prefer a score of 7 over rejecting potentially relevant work at the title-filtering stage; the abstract-level review should make the final decision.
