### World Models for Embodied Intelligence

The dominant theme across today's submissions is the maturation of world models from passive video predictors into executable, physically grounded simulators that close the loop with policy learning. A clear convergence is emerging around shared latent dynamics transformersthat jointly evolve visual, geometric, and action representations—exemplified by Thinker-WM's unified RGB-depth-action backbone and D-World's explicit, inspectable digital twin with recursive self-improvement under dual control. Several works attack the long-standinglong-horizon consistency problemfrom complementary angles: LongForcing identifies a supervision-horizon gap in autoregressive video diffusion and supervises extended student self-rollouts, while Directional Pullback Regularization introduces a kinematics-aware anisotropic latent metric for driving rollouts, and Order-Consistent Latent Geometry formalizes "self-approachingness" as a planner-facing compatibility condition linking latent dimension to angular capacity. Crucially, the field is moving towardreliable, benchmarked simulators: WorldOracle provides the first systematic diagnostic comparing action-conditioned world models against paired real-world rollouts along physical correctness, spatial alignment, and multi-view coherence, while WoVR+ and Trust-Calibrated VLA post-training both use hallucination-aware or reliability-gated imagined rollouts to make RL on VLA policies safe and effective. Physics grounding is deepening beyond pixels—Code2Worlds-2 compiles generated 4D worlds into physics-engine-loadable SimReady scenes, and Pixels to Executable Physics grounds video generation in reconstructed terrain contact dynamics. Finally, a notable strand interrogateswhat actually matters: What Limits Cross-Task Generalization decouples world model from inverse dynamics and shows that a vision-to-action loss redirecting future generation toward action-relevant detail is key, echoing Physics-Aware Object-Centric SE(3) Forecasting's argument that compact contact-relevant futures suffice over dense visual prediction.

World-Action Models and VLA Policy Learning

A second major direction concerns the tight coupling of world models with action generation, with several papers pushing towardlightweight, deployment-ready world-action models. WAVE-0 bridges VLA policies and world-action models via lightweight latent future prediction without pixel reconstruction or large-scale video pretraining, while StreamingWAM tackles the real-time control bottleneck through chunk-wise staircase flow matching and prefix rollout for cross-chunk continuity. LoopWAM addresses the practical reality of learning frommixed-quality deployment rolloutsvia source-dependent noise sampling and quality steering, and OccWAM introduces an occupancy-native formulation with joint scene-to-ego flow matching that enables reciprocal interaction between prediction and planning. On the policy-improvement side, ForkCast uses candidate-conditioned counterfactual futures to select recovery interventions for frozen VLA policies, ReEnvision maintains a joint future-action belief for selective action repair, and BELIEFWEAVER combines persistent relational belief states with an implicit world model for long-horizon failure recovery—together suggesting a shift from monolithic policies towardmodular anticipate-diagnose-repair loops. Data efficiency is also a focus: Act4R achieves kinematics-free control via action-conditioned 4D reconstruction, DR-VLA transfers egocentric human video to quadruped navigation through domain-invariant scene representations, and EgoMatrix converts human videos into physically faithful robot demonstrations. The emergence of Data-Free Continual Learning for world action models, which reveals that forgetting concentrates specifically in inverse dynamics, offers an important diagnostic for future continual-learning work in this space.

Video Generation and Generative Modeling

Today's video generation work is characterized byefficiency, controllability, and structural fidelityrather than raw scale. LinMorph improves Video DiT linearization via a learned softmax-to-linear weight morphism, and FlashVSR+ combines hybrid attention with reward-yield distribution matching for efficient super-resolution—both reflecting a broader push to distill large generative models without sacrificing quality. Consistency across long horizons remains a central challenge: CineMem introduces attention-aligned fine-grained memory retrieval with online-clustered KV caches for multi-shot generation, GAD transfers cross-frame correspondences from a frozen geometry model into video attention, and 4LC proposes a 4D latent cache reusing composite static-dynamic representations for efficient spatiotemporal control. Text alignment is strengthened by VARI's group-structured intent alignment with form-contrastive flow-DPO and FlowCred's hierarchical credit assignment for joint prompt-generator post-training. More specialized contributions include Multics' hierarchical communication for multi-player world models, hierarchical curriculum learning for surgical video, and H-DDCM's Hadamard codebooks for training-free generative compression. In image generation, PixelMoE scales pixel-space diffusion with LLM-style mixture-of-experts, and Diagnose-to-Generate addresses structural distortions via patch-level grounded alignment—both offering architectural ideas with plausible transfer to video.

1. Thinker-WM: A Unified World Model with Shared Multimodal Representations for Embodied Intelligence

ArXiv ID: 2610.18447v1 | Relevance Score: 9/10 | Novelty Score: 8/10

👥 Authors: Thinker Team, Peng Gao, Jichao Jiao, Chen Liao, Daqin Luo, Yiming Ma, Baiyu Pan, Hailin Shi, Jiyuan Wang, Xiangming Xu, Xiongwei Zhao

🏫 Affiliations: Unknown Institution

💡 Highlight: Unified multimodal world model jointly evolving RGB, metric depth, and robot action in a shared dynamics transformer, supporting action-conditioned generation, world prediction, and inverse dynamics.
View Abstract

World models have emerged as a promising paradigm for embodied AI, yet existing systems often remain fragmented in the modalities they model and the functions they support. In particular, visual dynamics, geometric structure, and executable action are commonly coupled through separate interfaces or downstream modules rather than being jointly represented and evolved within a shared dynamics model. In this work, we present Thinker-WM, a unified multimodal world model that jointly models and generates RGB, metric depth, and robot action through a shared intermediate Unified Dynamics Transformer with modality-specific encoders and decoders. The model performs multimodal unification at the representation level: appearance, geometry, and action are transformed into a common dynamic representation and jointly evolved by shared parameters, while their distinct output structures are preserved. The same parameterization supports multiple functions, including joint video-action generation, world prediction, policy inference, action-conditioned generation, video-to-action generation, and inverse dynamics. To examine whether the shared representation captures action-related information, we introduce a lightweight motion probe that shows the frozen visual representation contains recoverable motion information. Thinker-WM is trained on a heterogeneous corpus comprising approximately 20K hours of egocentric video, 10K hours of real-robot data, and 1K hours of simulation data, including approximately 4K hours of real-robot data with aligned depth supervision. Extensive experiments demonstrate strong performance across embodied world-model evaluations. Thinker-WM ranks first on EWMBench, WorldOlympiad and WorldArena, while controlled studies further demonstrate the benefits of action conditioning, geometric interaction, and multi-function training within the shared representation.

2. From Pixels to Executable Physics: Behavior-Grounded World Models for Embodied Action Consequence Prediction

ArXiv ID: 2610.17630v1 | Relevance Score: 9/10 | Novelty Score: 8/10

👥 Authors: Ruo Chen, Jiahua Pang, Yingjie Niu, Hongyi Lin, Jun Tian, Yang Zhu, Chao Li

🏫 Affiliations: Unknown Institution

💡 Highlight: Builds a physically consistent quadruped world model by grounding video generation in reconstructed terrain contact dynamics, enabling iterative rollouts and better action-consequence prediction.
View Abstract

In outdoor environments, varying terrain and contact conditions can cause identical action commands to produce different motion outcomes for quadruped robots. Predicting these outcomes is essential for reliable navigation and informed path selection. Existing video world models can generate visually plausible futures, yet often struggle to capture robot dynamics and terrain interactions, leading to overly optimistic predictions of action outcomes. We present DR-WM, a physically consistent world model that incorporates robot-terrain contact dynamics to predict the consequences of quadruped actions. Starting from an initial RGB image, our method reconstructs scene geometry and integrates dense, dimensionless priors over terrain contact properties, calibrated using real-world action-response data. The resulting terrain model supports the simulation of physics with spatially varying contact properties such as friction. Given the initial state of the robot and candidate action sequences, we simulate its future motion and use the resulting camera trajectories and rendered geometry to guide the training of a video world model. The generated future observations are then used to update the terrain reconstruction, enabling iterative model rollouts without additional real-world images. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DR-WM substantially outperforms geometry-only baselines and baselines with uniform physical parameters on key physical metrics characterizing action outcomes, while achieving the best performance among the evaluated baselines. These physically grounded predictions support more informed action selection: across 90 challenging scenarios, an 8B vision-language model augmented with DR-WM outperforms an unassisted 32B vision-language model on five of six evaluation metrics.

3. LongForcing: Scaling Temporal Manifold Matching for Long-Horizon World Rollout

ArXiv ID: 2610.18289v1 | Relevance Score: 9/10 | Novelty Score: 7/10

👥 Authors: Ziyu Zhu, Zhaoxu Sun, Fan Jiang, Mengchao Wang, Junfeng Ni, Ting-Bing Xu, Zhicheng Liu, Mu Xu

🏫 Affiliations: AMAP, Alibaba Group

💡 Highlight: Identifies a supervision-horizon gap in autoregressive video diffusion and introduces long-horizon temporal manifold matching that supervises extended student self-rollouts, improving long-horizon world rollout consistency and enabling 24-hour interactive rollouts.
View Abstract

Autoregressive video diffusion models have emerged as a foundation for long-horizon world rollout. However, when extended far beyond their training windows, these models suffer from progressive drift: visual quality deteriorates, motion becomes inconsistent, and object identity gradually collapses as generated frames are recursively reused as context. We study a supervision-horizon gap: short-horizon distillation leaves later rollout states without direct supervision. We show that matching short-window marginals need not determine long-horizon behavior, while longer on-policy trajectories expose delayed failures to training. This motivates temporal manifold matching at scale for long-horizon world models. Building on this insight, we introduce LongForcing, a distillation approach that scales teacher supervision to long student self-rollouts. LongForcing preserves the short-horizon teacher-forcing and causal ODE-distillation stages, while replacing the realscore teacher in the final distribution-matching stage with an extended-horizon bidirectional teacher. The long-horizon teacher supervises extended student self-rollouts using the standard distribution-matching formulation, without modifying the student architecture or increasing inference cost. We evaluate LongForcing on text-to-video generation and interactive action-conditioned world models, achieving a VBench Quality score of 85.52 and improving WorldRoamBench 60s action score by 23.9 points in first-person ablations. Qualitative stress tests further sample interactive rollouts up to 24 hours, far beyond the training horizon. These results suggest that scaling supervision along the temporal axis is a practical step toward long-horizon world models. The project website and code are at https://github.com/Fantasy-AMAP/LongForcing.

4. Order-Consistent Latent Geometry for Reliable Short-Horizon Planning in World Models

ArXiv ID: 2610.18570v1 | Relevance Score: 8/10 | Novelty Score: 8/10

👥 Authors: Junlin Wu, Wen Fan, Dandan Zhang

🏫 Affiliations: Unknown Institution

💡 Highlight: Identifies self-approachingness as a planner-facing compatibility condition for latent world-model planning, proving that acute bends in latent geometry cause candidate-order reversals and linking latent dimension to angular capacity.
View Abstract

Latent planners often rank candidate action sequences by terminal Euclidean distance to a goal latent. Exact agreement with transition distance would make this score ideal, but is stronger than planning requires: along a transition-shortest rollout, the essential requirement is that latent distance does not reverse true transition progress. We show that this pathwise no-reversal requirement is precisely the classical self-approaching condition. To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to identify self-approachingness as a planner-facing compatibility condition for terminal Euclidean latent planning and to connect its violation to an exact finite-horizon candidate-order reversal. Because visual world models predict discrete latent sequences, we analyze their polygonal interpolation. We prove that an acute bend creates a nonempty region in which terminal Euclidean distance ranks a transition-worse candidate ahead of one that makes genuine progress, even under exact rollouts. We further interpret latent dimension as angular capacity through a classical spherical-code bound. A controlled six-branch experiment, including a zero-padded three-dimensional baseline, validates the predicted ordering failure and separates angular arrangement from ambient dimensionality.

5. WAVE-0: Bridging VLA Policies and WAMs with Lightweight Latent Future Prediction

ArXiv ID: 2610.18568v1 | Relevance Score: 8/10 | Novelty Score: 7/10

👥 Authors: Haomin Wang, Anqiong Ji, Ke Wang, Zhongshan Liu, Ru Ying, Ran Zheng, Zhipeng Zhang

🏫 Affiliations: Unknown Institution

💡 Highlight: Lightweight latent world model that predicts future representations to condition a VLA action expert, unifying latent planning and future prediction without pixel reconstruction or large-scale video pretraining.
View Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies excel at generalist robotic manipulation but generate actions without explicitly modeling their environmental consequences. World action models (WAMs) incorporate future prediction, yet most rely on costly video generation, while latent alternatives either use predicted futures only as auxiliary targets or infer latent actions from suboptimal visual transitions through costly large-scale video pretraining. We introduce WAVE-0 (World-model-Augmented VLA Execution), a framework that bridges VLAs and WAMs through a lightweight latent world model. By connecting the semantic understanding of pretrained VLAs with explicit future prediction, WAVE-0 allows anticipated action consequences to directly guide action generation. Built on a pretrained VLA, its 257M world model translates VLM-generated latent action plans into predicted future representations that condition the action expert, unifying latent planning, future prediction, and action generation. The world model is trained directly on ground-truth robot actions with a counterfactual ranking objective, requiring neither pixel reconstruction nor large-scale video pretraining. WAVE-0 achieves success rates of 91.47% on RoboTwin 2.0 and 98.7% on LIBERO, along with 88.0%, 61.4%, and 75.0% on LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-Pro, and LIBERO-Para, respectively. On real-world tasks, it improves the success rate of $π_{0.5}$ from 38.3% to 73.3%. These results demonstrate strong manipulation performance and generalization across diverse distribution shifts.

6. VARI: Robust Text-to-Video Generation via Group-Structured Intent Alignment

ArXiv ID: 2610.18189v1 | Relevance Score: 8/10 | Novelty Score: 7/10

👥 Authors: Mingxuan Zhu, Hohei Chan, Shiyuan Liu, Yuanxing Zhang, Lu Chen

🏫 Affiliations: Unknown Institution

💡 Highlight: Group-structured intent alignment framework with group-consistent distillation and form-contrastive flow-DPO that improves text-to-video semantic robustness and cross-prompt-form consistency without inference-time modules.
View Abstract

Modern text-to-video (T2V) models generate visually compelling videos, yet meaning-preserving changes in prompt wording can substantially alter semantic realization and overall quality. Existing prompt rewriting modifies the inference input, whereas conventional post-training constructs supervision from one prompt formulation at a time; neither explicitly optimizes reliability across equivalent forms. We introduce VARI (Verified Alignment over Representations of Intent), a group-structured framework that treats automatically verified formulations of the same visual intent as a shared supervision unit. VARI builds matched form-seed rollout pools, cross-scores each video against all forms, and applies a wording-independent structured intent audit to identify valid realizations and hard semantic failures. Group-Consistent Distillation (GCD) aligns every verified form with interchangeable valid realizations, while Form-Contrastive Flow-DPO (FC-DPO) learns group-level preferences from audited positive-negative pairs, prioritizing same-seed, different-form contrasts. Research Interest: World Models and Better Video Generation

Curate papers that provide useful insight into learned world models OR introduce methods that can materially improve video generation. A paper does not need to be action-conditioned, interactive, or explicitly branded as a "world model" if its contribution clearly advances one of the two tracks below.

Track A: World Models and Visual Dynamics

Select substantive work on learned models of how environments evolve, especially when it contributes insight or techniques for:

  • visual/video world models, future prediction, or generative environment simulation;
  • action-conditioned, controllable, interactive, or closed-loop rollout;
  • physical, causal, spatial, temporal, or object-centric representations of dynamics;
  • long-horizon prediction, memory, state abstraction, uncertainty, generalization, planning, or embodied/robotic understanding;
  • learning world models from video, multimodal observations, latent actions, or weak supervision.

Viewable future frames are strongly preferred but are not mandatory when the paper offers a clear, broadly useful world-model insight. A latent-state world model is eligible when modeling dynamics is the central technical contribution, rather than merely a standard component inside an RL or policy pipeline.

Track B: Methods That Improve Video Generation

Select papers whose main technical contribution plausibly improves generated video in at least one important dimension:

  • visual quality, motion quality, temporal consistency, identity/object persistence, or long-video coherence;
  • physical plausibility, causal consistency, compositional generalization, or dynamic scene understanding;
  • controllability through text, images, trajectories, cameras, actions, layouts, depth, or other meaningful conditions;
  • architectures, objectives, representations, tokenizers, diffusion/flow/autoregressive formulations, training strategies, inference methods, or data-curation methods designed for video generation;
  • generation efficiency, memory efficiency, real-time capability, or scaling methods with a concrete video-specific idea;
  • unified video understanding and generation when the generative capability is a substantial part of the contribution.

Text-to-video, image-to-video, video prediction, and action-conditioned generation are all eligible. Prefer methods with insights transferable beyond a single narrow application or visual style.

Low-priority cases

The following cases are usually lower priority, but do not reject them automatically. Include them when they have a meaningful connection to learned dynamics or video generation; assign a low relevance score only when that connection is absent:

  • video editing, restoration, compression, retrieval, captioning, recognition, tracking, segmentation, or understanding without a substantive generative/dynamics contribution;
  • human-motion, talking-head, avatar, or animation systems whose contribution is narrowly application-specific and offers no broadly useful video-generation insight;
  • 3D/4D reconstruction, novel-view synthesis, or neural rendering without a meaningful temporal generative-model contribution;
  • robot policies, VLA models, model-based RL, or autonomous-driving systems that only use an off-the-shelf world/video model as an auxiliary component;
  • datasets, benchmarks, surveys, evaluations, demos, or scaling reports without a substantive new method or a genuinely important empirical/conceptual insight;
  • generic image generation methods with no explicit video, temporal, dynamics, or world-model relevance.
