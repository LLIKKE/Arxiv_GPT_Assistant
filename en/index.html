💡 Daily Research Summary

### World Models & Predictive Dynamics

The dominant theme today is the maturation of world models from passive video predictors into controllable, action-conditioned simulators with persistent internal state. A clear architectural convergence is emerging around decoupling abstract state from visual state: StateCraft maintains an editable symbolic state to condition a video diffusion model, while Thinker-WM and Atomic-WAM jointly evolve RGB, depth, and action within shared dynamics transformers, and RepForcing shows that supervising the representation trajectory—rather than intermediate teacher outputs—yields better few-step autoregressive distillation. Equally notable is the turn toward execution-grounded and weakness-targeted training: Atomic-WAM uses GRPO rewards to force motion descriptions to be executable, and work on policy-conditioned environment generation synthesizes worlds aimed specifically at a policy's validated failure modes. Several papers attack the long-horizon consistency problem from complementary angles—decoupled drift-diffusion with heteroscedastic uncertainty gating, order-consistent latent geometry that proves acute bends in interpolated latent trajectories cause planning-order reversals, and occupancy modeling via one-step drifting that enables direct policy extraction through differentiation. Finally, the field is beginning to police itself: WorldMemBench reveals that static memory retention does not imply reliable state evolution, and PhyMetric supplies a scene-level QA protocol for diagnosing physical plausibility—together signaling that evaluation rigor is catching up to generation capability.

Embodied Agents & Vision-Language-Action Models

World modeling is being aggressively absorbed into the VLA stack, with today's papers converging on the question of where and how future prediction should enter the policy. A systematic disentanglement study of world-representation, integration-paradigm, and backbone choices provides transferable guidance, while ObjDream pushes toward object-centric temporal slots with action-conditioned future object-state prediction to improve manipulation generalization under distribution shift. In autonomous driving, SafeDriveVLA decouples conflict reasoning from action generation by rolling out strict navigation-following futures, and KinoGen uses kinematically grounded video generation to synthesize customizable humanoid loco-manipulation references with keyframe-level goal-pose control. A parallel thread targets sim-to-real transfer: Contact-UAN proposes a residual world model that corrects actuator dynamics while preserving simulator priors, and meta-learning approaches now actively select what to simulate for policy co-training. Complementing these methods, a survey on agentic robots organizes world models and world-action models into a broader taxonomy, and ASIWorld introduces a persistent survival sandbox for benchmarking sustained goal execution—evidence that the community is building both the conceptual map and the stress tests for embodied world-action systems.

Multimodal & Interactive Generation

Interactive generation is diversifying beyond RGB video into richer physical and perceptual modalities. SonoWorld brings pose-controllable ultrasound world models trained from freehand clinical video via latent-action pretraining and EM-based pose grounding, while PAWA targets multi-agent settings with a synchronized POV-plus-panoramic Minecraft dataset and cross-view token alignment. Physical fidelity is a recurring concern: interactive 4D volumetric liquid forecasting under moving-solid interaction introduces transport-aware coupling for long-horizon rollouts, and cardiac dynamic synthesis from sparse observations demonstrates a focus-align-diffuse keyframe paradigm for temporally coherent generation under weak temporal cues. On the efficiency and memory front, Kandinsky S2V 1.0 combines reference-aware multi-scale visual memory with pi-Flow distillation for long-form audio-driven avatars, and a world-state-centric framework improves cross-shot spatial and temporal consistency in agentic video generation. An intriguing cross-cutting insight comes from casting captioning and text-to-image generation as successive refinement with language as the low-rate code—a rate-distortion framing that may transfer directly to video generation objectives.

Evaluation, Benchmarks & Reliable Dynamics

A substantial cluster of work focuses on diagnosing rather than generating. IsoVRBench constructs isolated-intervention video preference pairs to train reward models without human feedback, improving both generation quality and temporal-order discrimination. Beyond video, VaryST extends spatio-temporal forecasting to open-world distribution shift via dynamic context construction and masking, RAN-JEPA separates representation learning from intervention learning to handle observation-intervention asymmetry in mobile network control, and dynamic graph world modeling predicts future latent node/relation states from temporal context—offering non-visual but transferable dynamics-prediction insights. Two papers probe the boundaries of what current systems can capture: an analysis of why 3D reconstruction breaks down on under-ice AUV video proposes fast failure detection, and work on physical time as a missing dimension of robot policy memory argues that memory states should evolve over measured time intervals rather than step counts. The throughline is a shift from benchmark scores toward mechanistic understanding of when and why learned dynamics models fail.

💡 Highlight: Introduces a causal, pose-controllable ultrasound video world model trained from freehand clinical video with latent-action pretraining, EM-based pose grounding, and distillation for long-horizon autoregressive rollout.

arXiv:2610.19327v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Robotic ultrasound can standardize image acquisition and reduce operator workload and variability, but policy learning remains limited by the lack of realistic, controllable simulation environments. While controllable world models have advanced rapidly in natural-image domains, ultrasound world modeling remains challenging because each frame captures only a cross-section of unobserved 3D anatomy, causing small probe motions to induce large appearance changes. Existing approaches rely on robot-tracked poses and latent-space prediction, but do not directly generate the observations required for interactive control. We propose SonoWorld, a causal ultrasound world model that learns video dynamics from unlabeled clinical videos and grounds them to 6-DoF control using a small set of freehand clinical scans tracked with an EM sensor. SonoWorld is trained in three stages: pretraining a latent-action-conditioned video diffusion model on around 6.9 million unlabeled multi-organ ultrasound frames; grounding the learned video dynamics to metric probe poses with a small EM-tracked thyroid dataset; and applying distribution matching distillation to enable causal, long-horizon autoregressive generation with selectively caching eviction tokens at inference time. We evaluate controllability through pose-following fidelity, round-trip consistency, and axis-specific control under synthetic actions on held-out tracked scans, and further validate it with a user study. In model-based action discrimination, SonoWorld correctly ranks the target-directed probe motion over an equal-magnitude opposite motion in up to 74.8% of trials. To our knowledge, SonoWorld is the first causal, pose-controllable ultrasound world model learned primarily from freehand clinical videos, providing a data-driven interactive environment for robotic ultrasound data acquisition and policy evaluation.

🏫 Affiliations: INSAIT Institute

💡 Highlight: Decouples a persistent, editable abstract state from the visual state to condition a video diffusion world model, improving long-horizon consistency and controllability in interactive generation.
View Abstract

arXiv:2610.19795v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Video world models aim to simulate complex environments with realistic appearance and dynamics. Maintaining consistency over long interactions requires tracking past actions and understanding how those actions affect the environment. Video world models can only generate partial observations of the entire world at a time. Remembering past observations alone may not be sufficient in every scenario, as they may no longer reflect the world after a sequence of actions. We introduce a paradigm that decouples a persistent, updatable abstract state from the visual state. The abstract state keeps track of the entities' changes as the world evolves, and is used to condition the visual state, which represents the current view. We propose StateCraft, a novel architecture that pairs a language model with a video diffusion model to evolve the dynamic world. We evaluate our approach on generative interfaces, which pose a substantial challenge because a single action may change parts of the state that are not currently visible. To support training and evaluation, we develop a data collection pipeline that produces informative, densely annotated trajectories at scale without human or agent supervision, together with a benchmark measuring generation quality, memory, and controllability. On this benchmark, our approach improves surpasses the state-of-the-art baselines. We demonstrate that StateCraft maintains high self-consistency and strong generation quality over long trajectories. Because the abstract state is explicit, it can be edited directly, and generation follows edits to states never observed in the data. Finally, a proof-of-concept 3D experiment, in which actions change the illumination of an unobserved room, shows that the paradigm can also be applied to spatial environments. Extensive experiments demonstrate strong performance across embodied world-model evaluations. Thinker-WM ranks first on EWMBench, WorldOlympiad and WorldArena, while controlled studies further demonstrate the benefits of action conditioning, geometric interaction, and multi-function training within the shared representation.

4. Decoupled Drift-Diffusion World Models for Consistent Future Frame Prediction

ArXiv ID: 2610.19212 | Relevance Score: 9/10 | Novelty Score: 8/10

👥 Authors: Anton Arapin, Aditya Singh, Yixin Wang, Jun Gao, Bernadette Bucher

🏫 Affiliations: Unknown Institution

💡 Highlight: Introduces a decoupled drift-diffusion world model that regresses deterministic next-state predictions plus a heteroscedastic uncertainty map to spatially gate a conditional diffusion process, improving action-conditioned future frame prediction.
View Abstract

arXiv:2610.19212v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: World models of visual information predict future image frames conditioned on given action sequences. This predicted scene evolution is driven by deterministic changes such as controlled motion on a robot platform, as well as stochastic changes such as rendering of manipulated objects or previously occluded regions. Our key insight is that the spatial location of these stochastic changes is itself learnable. We leverage this insight by jointly regressing both the deterministic next-state prediction and the spatial uncertainty map, then using the uncertainty map to guide a conditional diffusion process. We propose Decoupled Drift-Diffusion World Model (D3WM), which uses a transformer trained with a Gaussian negative-log-likelihood objective to produce both the deterministic next-state prediction and a spatial map of heteroscedastic aleatoric uncertainty. The learned uncertainty map then provides a structured spatial gate for a conditional diffusion model: deterministic regions are anchored to the predicted state, while uncertain regions, such as disocclusions or dynamic textures, are sampled by the diffusion process. We frame this decomposition under a variance-gated stochastic differential equation that separates deterministic drift from stochastic diffusion. D3WM shows improved results over competitive baselines in two distinct types of action spaces: robotic tabletop manipulation and camera motion.

arXiv:2610.20552v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We present KinoGen, a framework that synthesizes customizable humanoid loco-manipulation references through kinematically grounded video generation. KinoGen conditions a pretrained video generator on calibrated renders of the target robot and object in a simulated task instance and recovers the reference by fitting the same models to the generated two-view video, so that the reference lies in the robot configuration space by construction without a human body proxy or retargeting. arXiv:2610.17584v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Operational mobile networks are rich in observations but poor in interventions: they continuously generate large volumes of passive telemetry, while controlled action--response trajectories remain comparatively scarce and costly to collect. This observation--intervention asymmetry is a central obstacle to building practical world models for RAN control. We address this problem with RAN-JEPA, an action-conditioned world model that separates representation learning from intervention learning. The model first learns a predictive latent representation from observation-only network data, adapts that representation to the target deployment domain, and then learns action-conditioned latent dynamics from controlled sleep/wake trajectories while keeping the encoder fixed. We evaluate RAN-JEPA using public operator performance data for pretraining and a real-equipment four-cell 5G testbed for domain adaptation, post-training, and control. What-if evaluation further shows that the model produces distinct and coherent future trajectories under matched SLEEP and KEEP plans. In closed-loop control, RAN-JEPA consistently supports higher sleep ratios than the two baselines at comparable congestion cost. These results show that observation-first, action-conditioned learning can turn abundant passive telemetry and limited intervention data into a practical predictive model for energy-aware RAN control.

arXiv:2610.20111v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Distilling pretrained video diffusion models into few-step autoregressive (AR) generators typically combines student initialization with distribution matching distillation (DMD). An intermediate AR teacher can provide causally compatible targets for ODE initialization. However, causally compatible targets alone do not ensure high-quality generation under a short sampling schedule. We analyze the posterior-mean predictions favored by direct latent regression and empirically find that causal ODE initialization alleviates blur but still leaves fine detail unresolved. To address this limitation, we propose Representation Forcing, which learns few-step causal initialization through representation-space supervision rather than intermediate-teacher trajectory regression. The student is trained with teacher forcing by comparing clean predictions and training targets through normalized features of a frozen video diffusion transformer. This phase reuses the synthetic videos generated by a bidirectional model for AR teacher training and replaces both teacher training and causal ODE distillation, without generating additional causal ODE trajectories. Research Interest: World Models and Better Video Generation

Curate papers that provide useful insight into learned world models OR introduce methods that can materially improve video generation. A paper does not need to be action-conditioned, interactive, or explicitly branded as a "world model" if its contribution clearly advances one of the two tracks below.

Track A: World Models and Visual Dynamics

Select substantive work on learned models of how environments evolve, especially when it contributes insight or techniques for:

  • visual/video world models, future prediction, or generative environment simulation;
  • action-conditioned, controllable, interactive, or closed-loop rollout;
  • physical, causal, spatial, temporal, or object-centric representations of dynamics;
  • long-horizon prediction, memory, state abstraction, uncertainty, generalization, planning, or embodied/robotic understanding;
  • learning world models from video, multimodal observations, latent actions, or weak supervision.

Viewable future frames are strongly preferred but are not mandatory when the paper offers a clear, broadly useful world-model insight. A latent-state world model is eligible when modeling dynamics is the central technical contribution, rather than merely a standard component inside an RL or policy pipeline.

Track B: Methods That Improve Video Generation

Select papers whose main technical contribution plausibly improves generated video in at least one important dimension:

  • visual quality, motion quality, temporal consistency, identity/object persistence, or long-video coherence;
  • physical plausibility, causal consistency, compositional generalization, or dynamic scene understanding;
  • controllability through text, images, trajectories, cameras, actions, layouts, depth, or other meaningful conditions;
  • architectures, objectives, representations, tokenizers, diffusion/flow/autoregressive formulations, training strategies, inference methods, or data-curation methods designed for video generation;
  • generation efficiency, memory efficiency, real-time capability, or scaling methods with a concrete video-specific idea;
  • unified video understanding and generation when the generative capability is a substantial part of the contribution.

Text-to-video, image-to-video, video prediction, and action-conditioned generation are all eligible. Prefer methods with insights transferable beyond a single narrow application or visual style.

Low-priority cases

The following cases are usually lower priority, but do not reject them automatically. Include them when they have a meaningful connection to learned dynamics or video generation; assign a low relevance score only when that connection is absent:

  • video editing, restoration, compression, retrieval, captioning, recognition, tracking, segmentation, or understanding without a substantive generative/dynamics contribution;
  • human-motion, talking-head, avatar, or animation systems whose contribution is narrowly application-specific and offers no broadly useful video-generation insight;
  • 3D/4D reconstruction, novel-view synthesis, or neural rendering without a meaningful temporal generative-model contribution;
  • robot policies, VLA models, model-based RL, or autonomous-driving systems that only use an off-the-shelf world/video model as an auxiliary component;
  • datasets, benchmarks, surveys, evaluations, demos, or scaling reports without a substantive new method or a genuinely important empirical/conceptual insight;
  • generic image generation methods with no explicit video, temporal, dynamics, or world-model relevance.

Scoring Guidance

Treat the two tracks as alternatives, not requirements that must both be satisfied.

  • 8: clear and substantial contribution to either world modeling/visual dynamics or video-generation quality, consistency, control, efficiency, or training.
  • 9: unusually strong contribution that addresses a central limitation and has broad implications for world models or video generation.
  • 10: rare, potentially field-shaping work.
  • 6-7: meaningfully adjacent or useful, but indirect, narrow, incremental, or insufficiently supported by the abstract.
  • 0-5: weak overlap, auxiliary use, or largely outside the interests above.

Set RELEVANCE=true for papers scoring at least 5 that have a clear or plausible connection to either track. Relevance is the only hard selection requirement. Do not require explicit action conditioning, interactive rollout, visible frame prediction, a novel method, or a field-defining contribution. When evidence is incomplete but the title or abstract suggests a connection, include the paper with a cautious score of 5. Only reject papers that are clearly unrelated.
